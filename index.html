Overview.

J1 Fog is a C++ plugin that turns an Unreal level into a survival-horror fog world. The fog glows by itself and is lit by a gloomy sky. It is thicker near the ground and thinner above.

Big fog banks are anchored in the world and roll with the wind and its gusts, with clear gaps between them. Fog settles against walls and the ground. Strands drift round the player and part as he walks through them.

Around the fog come a flashlight, lamps with halos, world states from day and night to the rust-red Nightmare, and a film look. The film look locks exposure, keeps blacks deep and adds grain, a vignette and 2.35:1 bars.

Install.

Requirements

  • Unreal Engine 5.8. The module comes built for UE 5.8 on Windows 64-bit.
  • Volumetric Fog. J1 Fog looks best with Lumen. New UE 5.8 projects have both on.
  • Mesh Distance Fields, for fog that clings to walls. Lumen projects have them on.
  • Visual Studio with C++, to package a game (Unreal builds J1 Fog into it) or if Unreal asks to rebuild the plugin.

Install from Fab

  1. Install to your engine

    In Fab or the Epic Games Launcher's library, choose Install to Engine and pick Unreal Engine 5.8.

  2. Enable it in the project

    Open the project, go to Edit > Plugins, search for J1 Fog, tick it and restart the editor.

  3. Find the content

    The materials and presets are in J1 Fog Content in the Content Browser. If you don't see it, turn on Show Plugin Content in the Content Browser's settings.

Install a build by hand

  1. Close the editor

    A plugin can't be added to a running project.

  2. Copy the plugin

    Copy the J1Fog folder into <YourProject>/Plugins/. Create Plugins if it does not exist.

  3. Open the project

    The built module is in J1Fog/Binaries. If your engine build differs and Unreal asks to rebuild J1Fog, press Yes.

Packaged games

J1 Fog loads some of its materials, textures and sounds by name while the game runs, so it adds its own content folders to the cook. While the editor runs, Project Settings > Packaging > Additional Asset Directories to Cook lists them: /J1Fog/Materials, /J1Fog/Presets and the rest. There is nothing to set up: a packaged game has the full fog. The demo in /J1Fog/Demo is cooked only when your game opens one of its levels, and the Movie Render Queue preset in /J1Fog/Render stays out of the game.

J1 Fog builds for Windows 64-bit, in Development and Shipping.

Blueprint-only projects. A packaged game needs J1 Fog's code built into it, so Unreal builds the project's own game when you package, with Visual Studio. If you copied J1 Fog into your project's Plugins folder by hand, a Blueprint-only project packages Unreal's ready-made game instead, which has no J1 Fog in it: add any C++ class to the project once (Tools > New C++ Class), and the packaged game gets J1 Fog.

The first cook takes longer. It compiles J1 Fog's shaders for every graphics API the project targets. The DirectX 11 (SM5) versions of the cloud and cinematic-mass shaders are slow to compile: in a fresh project on a laptop the first cook took about 18 minutes, most of it these shaders. Later cooks reuse them. A game that ships on DirectX 12 only can drop DirectX 11 under Project Settings > Platforms > Windows > Targeted RHIs.

Quick start.

From a level with no fog to a fog world in a few minutes:

  1. Open a level

    with a floor and a few walls. Fog reads best against geometry.

  2. Place the director

    In Place Actors, search for J1 Fog Director and drag one into the level. Put it at ground height: the ground fog starts there.

  3. Pick a world state

    In its Details, under J1 Fog, set Preset to DA_J1Fog_MilkyStreet.

  4. Keep the lighting

    Leave J1 Fog > World > Lighting on Overcast sky (recommended). On a level with Ultra Dynamic Sky or a similar sky system, the director has already chosen Keep level lights.

  5. Press Play

    The final look appears only in Play. Press F for the flashlight.

  6. Only a thin haze?

    Volumetric fog is off. Press Fix in the editor's notification, or open Tools > J1 Fog > Fog Doctor.

  7. Add a fog mass

    Place a J1 Fog Pocket in an alley or across a road and scale its box. At the top of its Details, press Column between walls or Ankle Deep on a road.

  8. Make it yours

    Duplicate the preset and set the copy on the director. Press Play and move its sliders; changes show at once.

Tip

The Mood Library in Tools > J1 Fog shows every preset with a thumbnail. On open landscape without a sky, try Lighting Sun, sky and clouds with Sunset. The viewport then shows the whole look.

Tip

To see J1 Fog at work first, open /J1Fog/Demo/L_J1Fog_DemoTown, a small foggy town with pockets, a fog river, lamps and zones, and press Play. L_J1Fog_DemoTour walks a camera through its zones by itself.

05 / 23

The Director.

The J1 Fog Director turns the level into a fog world. Place exactly one, at ground height: the ground fog, the banks and the height tints are measured from it. Blueprint finds it with Get Fog Director.

Its Preset holds the base world state. Leave Preset empty to use the director's own Settings instead.

Lighting

J1 Fog > World > Lighting decides who lights the fog.

LightingWhat happens
Overcast sky (recommended)In Play the director lights the world with its own overcast sky and sky dome. The level's sky light, sun, sky atmosphere and clouds stay off until Play ends.
Keep level lightsThe level's sky light and sun stay, and the fog's colour depends on them. The world state still sets the exposure (Lock Exposure), so match it to the level's light: night states on a daylit level come out white.
Sun, sky and cloudsThe director brings its own sun, sky atmosphere, drifting clouds and sky light. The world state sets them, in the editor and in Play.

Sun, sky and clouds is for levels without a sky of their own; any sky the level has is hidden in Play. With Preview Full Look (on), the editor shows the whole look too.

Changing the world state

Set Base Preset (New Preset, Blend Time, Transition) switches the state at runtime. Blend Time 0 is a hard cut; 10–30 s is fog rolling in. The defaults are 3 s and Staged.

TransitionHow it unfolds
Staged (default)Wind and sky turn first, then the horizon haze thickens. The fog closes in, and lamps and grade come last.
EvenEverything changes together: a plain crossfade.
FrontSky and wind turn first, then the new fog rolls in as a wall. Clearing fog leaves as a clear edge.
DriftFor weather over minutes or the time of day. Every part moves steadily in wide, overlapping windows.
SnapAt once, whatever the blend time. The eye still adapts.

A preset can reorder its Staged changes with a recipe. On Mood Change Started and On Mood Change Finished let your own music follow the change.

Fog fronts

With Front, the wind rises and the light dims, then a wall of fog rolls in from where the new state's wind comes from. It covers the camera and moves on; clearing fog leaves as a clear edge.

A front needs the fog's thickness to change by at least 15 %, and the foggier state must have less than 500 m of sight. Otherwise, and always for zones, pushed moods and the cinematic mood, the change runs as Staged.

Setting (J1 Fog > Front)DefaultWhat it does
Front Speed2500 cm/sHow fast the wall travels.
Front Reach30000 cmThe farthest the wall can start. The volumetric fog reaches this far while a front comes.
Front From0°Where a front comes from if the new state has no wind. 0 = from +X.

On Fog Front Arrived fires when the wall reaches the camera: for a sound, a cutscene or enemies.

Sound

The director plays the wind loop S_J1Fog_Wind, louder as the wind rises. In a change the wind moves first, so you hear it before the fog arrives.

Replace the loop in Wind Sound or silence it with Wind Volume (0.5; 0 = off). Ambience Fade Time (4 s) crossfades the states' ambience loops.

Start and camera cuts

Setting (J1 Fog > Start)DefaultWhat it does
Fade In From Black0 s (off)Play starts on black, then fades in. 2 s is a good value.

Fade In From Black hides the first seconds, while Lumen, the volumetric fog and TSR build up their history. A game-made cut is a jump of 10 m or more in one frame, or a very fast turn; Sequencer cuts are left alone.

Showcase

In Play the director can cycle through the states in Showcase Presets by itself, for demo maps and store videos. Showcase Hold (4 s), Showcase Blend (10 s) and Showcase Transition (Staged) set the timing. Front works here.

Preview a change, capture a look

J1 Fog > Preview Change plays a change in the editor without Play. Its fields are not saved.

  1. Set Preview To, and Preview From if the change should not start from the base state.
  2. Pick Preview Transition (Front) and Preview Blend Time (12 s).
  3. Drag Preview Progress from 0 to 1 to watch the change, fronts included. Clear Preview To when you are done.

Capture Look As Preset saves the look on screen as a new preset in /Game/J1FogLooks. During Play, press it on the director in the Play world to save the live mix.

World states (presets).

A world state is one complete look, saved as an ordinary Data Asset. It can be the director's base state, a zone's mood, a pushed mood, a weather step or a cinematic mood. Distances are in cm, speeds in cm/s and colours in linear RGB.

The weather pack

/J1Fog/Presets/Weather holds 39 more states of one open place, a field with a lone house and a tree line: clear mornings, grey days, dawn and dusk, fog, rain, storms, nights and strange skies (the Eerie and Surge states). They are made for Lighting on Sun, sky and clouds; even the grey states and the nights keep a sun or a moon. Use them as the steps of a J1 Fog Weather.

Make your own

  • Duplicate a preset and tune it. In Play, changes show at once.
  • Create one: right-click in the Content Browser > Miscellaneous > Data Asset > J1FogPreset.
  • Capture the look on screen with Capture Look As Preset or Capture look.

The defaults below are those of a new preset.

The look lock

Each preset stores the J1 Fog look it was made with: Look Version, under J1 Fog > Look (advanced). Looks added by later versions stay off for it, so an update never changes a finished frame.

New presets, directors and zones get the newest look, Look Version 3. Shipped presets have Look Version 2 or 3, and in this version both show every look. The lock holds back only work saved with Look Version 1: there Backlight, Toward Sun Tint, Away From Sun Tint, Black Fog and Light Spread keep the values that draw the old picture. If one of these does nothing in a preset, press Upgrade Look.

Transition recipes

A recipe sets when each part of a Staged change moves. Put it in a preset's Arrival (group Change), and every Staged change to that state follows it. Create one with right-click > Miscellaneous > Data Asset > J1 Fog Transition Recipe.

Each part moves in a window from Start to End, as shares of the blend time, with an Ease. The defaults are Wind 0–0.35, Sky 0–0.7, Far Fog 0.15–0.7, Near Fog 0.3–0.9, Lamps 0.45–1 and Film 0.6–1.

Four recipes are ready in /J1Fog/Recipes: StormRollsIn, NightmareCut (already on the Nightmare preset), SlowHour and LightsOut.

Fog pockets.

J1 Fog Pocket

A pocket holds a mass of thick fog. Place it in an alley, a yard or across a road, and stretch and rotate it to fit. It needs a J1 Fog Director, or any volumetric fog, in the level.

The fog lies on the floor of the box in billows, drifts with the wind and spills along the ground. It shows in the viewport at once, so you can art-direct it like a light.

Fog Time From Sequence (Cinematic, off), or j1.Fog.SequenceTime 1, moves the fog on a playing sequence's time instead of the game's. Masses, wind, strands, rivers, lamps and reveals follow it; light, zone moods and the eye do not. When the sequence stops, the fog carries on without a jump.

Rendering with Movie Render Queue

In Movie Render Queue, use Load Preset to load MRQ_J1Fog_Cinematic from /J1Fog/Render. It renders deferred to PNG, with the fog on the sequence's time and 96 rendered warm-up frames before each shot. Change the output, resolution and samples as usual.

The warm-up runs on the shot's own frames when its camera cut starts before the render range (Use Camera Cut For Warm Up). Without a warm-up, the first frame is brighter and settles over a few seconds. For smooth video, render at a fixed frame rate, such as 30 fps.

Re-rendering part of a shot

Set the range with Custom Playback Range in the job's output settings. With fog time from the sequence, the fog matches the full render frame for frame. Start one second (30 frames) early, because light and TSR still settle, and drop those frames.

Density for the shot

A wide shot puts more fog between the camera and its subject than a close-up. With Shot Density (off), a Cine Camera's shot thins the fog by the distance to its subject. The subject is at the focus distance, or else in the middle of the frame.

SettingDefaultWhat it does

Cinematic masses

Cinematic Masses (Heterogeneous Volume) draws the masses near the camera as a Heterogeneous Volume. They come out finer and sharper, shade themselves and take the lights' shadows. Further out the volumetric fog takes over, over a soft seam.

Path tracer

The engine's path tracer does not draw volumetric fog, so J1's banks and pockets vanish. Cinematic masses show but 3–4 times brighter; clouds blur and darken, halos are missing, and the backlight lights the scene like a real light. Render J1 fog deferred.

Depth Planes

The fog has four planes. Near strands are the strands, veils and particles; Middle masses are the banks, clinging fog, pockets, rivers and painted fog. Far wall is the base fog and far haze, and the fourth plane is the Sky.

Each plane has Solo, Hide and Lock. Solo and Hide work in the viewport and in Play but are never saved, so a level never ships with a hidden plane. The sky hides only in Play.

Sky systems (Ultra Dynamic Sky).

A sky system is an actor with its own sky: at least three of a sun, sky light, atmosphere, volumetric clouds and height fog. J1 Fog recognises one by itself, Ultra Dynamic Sky included.

A director placed on such a level sets itself up. Lighting goes to Keep level lights, and Level Sky Color All States (World group) is ticked at 1. The fog then takes the sky's colour in every state, so dawn, dusk and night colour it.

If the director was set up differently, the editor shows Set up for this sky, and the doctor has the same fix. It works in one Ctrl+Z step: Keep level lights, the sky's colour in the fog, and J1 Fog Weather on the sky's Time of Day.

The sky system's height fog stays in the level; in Play the director hides it and takes its colour. With Level Sky Color All States off, each preset's own Level Sky Color applies instead: 1 follows the level's sky, 0 keeps the state's own.

Keep level lights needs a movable Sky Light, and the engine draws only one cloud layer; the doctor checks both.

Known limits.

What J1 Fog 1.0 does not do, or does differently from what you might expect:

  • Cutters, anchors and reveals leave the level's thin base fog, which is the engine's own height fog.
  • Without volumetric fog, only the height haze is left.
  • The path tracer does not draw volumetric fog.
  • The engine draws only one volumetric cloud layer.
  • A front's wall moves as a plane and does not follow the terrain.
  • In multiplayer, changes of state are not synchronised between players.
  • Weather sound plays all around the camera, not from a place, and there is no zone for sound alone: a zone changes the ambience only with its Fog part.
  • Cinematic masses have no clinging fog inside and are too heavy for gameplay.
  • The brush does not paint on very steep walls.
  • Painted fog is not stirred by interactors, cut by cutters or counted by sensors.
  • Visibility Limit reaches no further than Volume Distance.
  • The director draws only the 32 newest stirs.
  • Silhouette Backing works on the four nearest pawns 5–80 m away.
